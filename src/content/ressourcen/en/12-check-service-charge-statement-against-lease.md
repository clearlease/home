What can be passed on is not decided by the statement. It is decided by the valid lease, with all its amendments. In commercial leases, only what has been agreed can be recharged. Many leases refer to § 2 BetrKV for this, others list the items themselves.

## If you prepare the statement

- **Items:** Is every cost type in the lease or in an amendment? Management and maintenance only belong if they were agreed.
- **Allocation key:** Which key applies to which item, and did an amendment change it?
- **Caps:** Is there a cap or a flat rate, and does it still apply?
- **Areas:** Does the rented area match the lease, including changes made by amendment?
- **Property tax:** Is it agreed as an item? It is listed in § 2 no. 1 BetrKV, but without an agreement it stays with the landlord.
- **CO2 costs:** For non-residential buildings, the tenant may bear 50 per cent at most (§ 8 CO2KostAufG). Is your split correct?

## If you receive the statement

Check the same points for each tenancy: Is the item agreed? Do key, area and cap fit? If in doubt, ask for the supporting documents before you pay.

## Why this fails across a portfolio

Checking a statement against the lease means knowing the valid state. It is spread across the original lease and its amendments, site by site. With many properties, there is no time for it. The result: Mineko found deviations in 84% of the commercial statements it audited (2026 analysis, only statements that tenants chose to have audited). Where deviations occur, they typically amount to 3 to 4 € per m² and year (clearlea.se estimate).
